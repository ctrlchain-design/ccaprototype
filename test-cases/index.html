TC-01
Explore the New Fleet and Driver Menu
📍 Phase 2 · Fleet and Driver Management, New Menu · Free exploration of the new interface first impressions, orientation, and understanding of the two main tabs
Transport Manager

Objective

Understand participants' first impressions of the new Fleet and Driver menu before they complete any tasks. Surface how they orient themselves, what they notice first, and whether the two main tabs, Set Availability and Manage Details, are immediately understood without guidance.

Starting Point

Fleet and Driver → Menu landing (no specific tab selected)

Task Prompt, read aloud

🎙 Say to participant

"CtrlChain just released a new menu called Fleet and Driver Management. Let's take a look at it together. Have a look at the two views inside: Set Availability and Manage Details."

Observe

Where does the participant look first: the tabs, the table, the actions?
Does the participant explore all three resource tabs (Vehicle, Trailer, Driver) without being prompted?
Does the participant switch between the Set Availability and Manage Details tabs on their own?
Does the participant seem to understand the purpose of each tab, or do they appear confused about the distinction?
Any moment of hesitation, surprise, or delight during exploration?

Follow up Questions

What is your first impression of this menu? What stood out to you?
You saw the Set Availability and Manage Details tabs. In your own words, what do you think each one is for? Can you tell the difference between them?
Is there anything you would organise or label differently?
TC-02
Add a New Vehicle and Assign It to a Carrier Group
📍 Phase 2 · Fleet and Driver Management, New Menu · Add resources and assign to the correct carrier subgroup
Transport Manager

Objective

Verify that users understand the overall logic of the Fleet and Driver menu, that it is split into Vehicle, Trailer, and Driver tabs, and that adding a resource here is how you make it manageable in the system. Also test whether the Carrier Group visibility section is understandable and users know what it controls.

Starting Point

Fleet and Driver → Vehicle tab

Task Prompt, read aloud

🎙 Say to participant

"A new truck has joined the NewCold Transport Poland fleet. Its licence plate is PL WW 4521 and it is a Semi Tractor. Please add it to the system and make sure it is only assigned to the Poland entity, it should not be shared with any other group in the enterprise."

Success Criteria

Participant finds the add button on the Vehicle tab without hesitation
Participant fills in licence plate (PL WW 4521) and vehicle type (Semi Tractor) correctly
Participant locates the Carrier Group visibility section and assigns the vehicle to NewCold Transport Poland only
Participant does NOT share the vehicle with other groups, understands scope restriction
Participant can explain in their own words what the Carrier Group field controls
Participant saves the vehicle successfully

Observe

Does the participant understand the menu structure (Vehicle / Trailer / Driver tabs) immediately, or do they seem unsure?
Is the add button easy to find, or does the participant scan the whole page first?
Does the participant notice the Carrier Group visibility section, or scroll past it?
Does the participant understand that only Poland means NOT shared enterprise-wide?
Any confusion about which form fields are required vs optional?

Follow up Questions

Looking at the Vehicle, Trailer, and Driver tabs, does this structure make sense? Is anything missing?
What would happen if you shared this vehicle with the entire enterprise instead?
Is there any information you expected to fill in but didn't see on the form?
TC-03
Delete a Vehicle No Longer Used by the Carrier Group
📍 Phase 2 · Fleet and Driver Management, Vehicle Management · Remove a decommissioned vehicle from the system; verify how deleted resources are presented
Transport Manager

Objective

Verify that users can find and delete a vehicle that has been decommissioned or is no longer active in the carrier group. Test whether the delete action is discoverable, whether a confirmation step is clear, and whether the way the system handles deleted records (hidden, archived, or visible as inactive) makes sense to the participant.

Starting Point

Fleet and Driver → Vehicle tab

Task Prompt, read aloud

🎙 Say to participant

"Vehicle BE 222 QW has been permanently decommissioned and will no longer be used by NewCold Transport Poland. Please remove it from the system."

Success Criteria

Participant finds vehicle BE 222 QW in the Vehicle tab
Participant finds the delete action for the vehicle (not the edit or unavailability action)
Participant completes the deletion (confirms the dialog if one appears)
Participant can describe what they expect to happen to the record after deletion

Observe

Is the delete action easy to find, does the participant locate it without exploring multiple areas?
Does the participant hesitate before deleting, do they look for a confirmation step?
Does the participant expect the vehicle to disappear entirely, or to appear as archived/greyed out?
After deletion, does the participant check whether the vehicle still appears somewhere in the list?
Does the participant confuse "delete" with "deactivate" or "mark unavailable"?

Follow up Questions

How easy was it to find the delete option, was it where you expected?
What would be the reason that you need to look for a deleted vehicle and how do you expect to see those data?
TC-04
Hide a Vehicle from Trip Planner Using the Visibility Toggle
📍 Phase 2 · Fleet and Driver Management, Carrier Group Visibility · Toggle a vehicle off from Trip Planner so planners cannot accidentally assign a resource that is not available for general planning
Transport Manager

Objective

Verify that a transport manager can find and use the Trip Planner visibility toggle to hide a vehicle from planners. Test whether the setting is discoverable, whether it is clear what the toggle controls, and whether the manager understands the impact of hiding a resource.

Starting Point

Fleet and Driver → Vehicle tab → ES 654 AA

Task Prompt, read aloud

🎙 Say to participant

"Vehicle ES 654 AA is on a dedicated contract with a key customer and must not appear as an option when planners assign vehicles in Trip Planner. Please make sure it is hidden from planners."

Success Criteria

Participant finds the Trip Planner visibility toggle on the vehicle record
Participant understands what the toggle does before switching it
Participant switches the toggle to hide the vehicle from Trip Planner
Participant can explain what would change for planners after this action

Observe

Does the participant find the visibility toggle without being guided, or do they look in the wrong section first?
Does the label of the toggle make its effect clear before they switch it?
Does the participant look for a confirmation or a way to verify the change took effect?
Does the participant express any concern about the vehicle disappearing from planner view entirely?

Follow up Questions

Was the toggle where you expected it, or did you have to look for it?
Before you switched it, was it clear what would happen?
In your day to day work, how often would you need to hide a vehicle from planners?
Phase 3

Basic Resource Availability

Full day unavailability blocks for drivers, vehicles, and trailers. Planners see availability when assigning in Trip Planner and Trips. No partial day or approval flow yet, those are Phase 4.

Fleet and Driver, Add Events: mark as unavailable with reason and end date (full day blocks)
Trip Planner: see unavailability when assigning via drawer, drag & drop, right click, or auto-assign
Trip Planner: alert when an already-assigned resource becomes unavailable
Trip Actions: unavailability warnings in trip details when assigning resources
TC-05
Record an Unavailability Event on a Vehicle
📍 Phase 3 · Fleet and Driver, Add Events · Record an open-ended unavailability event on the scheduler for a vehicle that is out of service
Transport Manager

Objective

Using the vehicle just added in TC-02, verify that users can find it in the list and record an unavailability event on the Fleet and Driver scheduler. Also test whether the resource list is easy to navigate and whether its display logic makes sense to the participant.

Starting Point

Fleet and Driver → Vehicle tab (PL WW 4521 already in list from TC-02)

Task Prompt, read aloud

🎙 Say to participant

"Vehicle PL WW 4521 is going in for a long-term repair starting today (Thursday, 16/07), with no end date. Please make sure this unavailability event is recorded on the Fleet and Driver menu scheduler."

Success Criteria

Participant finds vehicle PL WW 4521 in the list without excessive searching
Participant locates the option to add an unavailability event on the scheduler
Participant sets today as the start date and leaves end date open / no end date
The event is visibly recorded on the vehicle's row in the Fleet and Driver scheduler

Observe

How does the participant find PL WW 4521, scroll, search, or sort? Easy or frustrating?
Does the participant understand how to add an event on the scheduler, where to click?
Does the participant look for confirmation that the event was recorded on the vehicle?
Does the participant try the Trip Planner toggle instead of an event, are the two mechanisms confusing?

Follow up Questions

How easy was it to find vehicle PL WW 4521 in the list? What would make it easier?
Looking at this list of vehicles, what is the best way to show and organise resources here? (sorting, grouping, filters)
Is the information on the scheduler (event card, pop up) enough to summarize what unavailability events have been added?
TC-06
Close an Open-Ended Unavailability Event by Adding an End Date
📍 Phase 3 · Fleet and Driver, Events · Edit an existing open-ended event to add a specific end date, making it close-ended
Transport Manager

Objective

Verify that users can find and edit an existing open-ended unavailability event to add a specific end date. Test whether the edit action is discoverable, whether it is clear the user is modifying an existing event (not adding a new one), and whether setting an end date feels intuitive.

Starting Point

Fleet and Driver → Vehicle tab (PL WW 4521 has the open-ended repair event recorded in TC-04)

Task Prompt, read aloud

🎙 Say to participant

"Good news: the mechanic just called and the repair on PL WW 4521 finished earlier than expected. The vehicle will be ready to use tomorrow morning (Friday, 17/07 at 08:00). Please update the unavailability record to reflect this."

Success Criteria

Participant finds the existing open-ended event on PL WW 4521
Participant opens the edit action (not creating a new event)
Participant sets tomorrow as the end date and saves the updated event
The event now shows a close-ended date range on the scheduler

Observe

Does the participant reach for an edit action, or do they try to delete the event and add a new one?
Is it obvious where to set or change the end date in the edit form?
Does the participant understand that "tomorrow morning" means setting today as the end date?
After saving, does the participant check that the event now shows the correct end date on the record?

Follow up Questions

Was the edit option where you expected it, or did you have to look for it?
TC-07
Assign a Vehicle to a Trip Using the Trip Drawer
📍 Phase 3 · Trip Planner, Assign Resources Drawer · Open the assign resources drawer from a trip; select a vehicle while PL WW 4521 appears unavailable in the list
Planner

Objective

Understand how planners use the Assign Resources drawer to select a vehicle for a trip. Surface usability issues with the drawer: whether the page makes sense at first glance, how the planner knows which vehicle type is needed, and whether the information shown is enough to make a confident assignment when one vehicle in the list is unavailable.

Starting Point

Trip Planner → Trips section (TRIP2026-015573 is the first trip in the list; ES 771 BB is visible near the top of the vehicle dropdown and is marked unavailable)

Task Prompt, read aloud

🎙 Say to participant

"Let's go to the Trip Planner. Can you find trip TRIP2026-015573 in the Trips section and open its resources panel? From there, please assign vehicle ES 771 BB to the trip."

Success Criteria

Participant clicks the trip and opens the Assign Resources drawer
Participant opens the vehicle dropdown and scans the options
Participant notices that ES 771 BB is unavailable (sees the indicator without being told)
Participant selects an available vehicle and proceeds to assign

Observe

Does the Assign Resources page make sense immediately, or does the participant pause to figure out what to do?
How does the participant decide which vehicle to pick? Do they look for vehicle type, licence plate, or something else?
Does the participant notice ES 771 BB is unavailable, or do they try to select it anyway?
Does the drawer show enough context (vehicle type, owner, plate, availability) to assign with confidence, or does the participant feel they are missing information?
Does the participant look for additional details about the trip (cargo type, route) to inform their choice, and are those details visible?

Follow up Questions

Looking at this the assign resources drawer, does it make sense to you? Is there anything confusing or anything you would change?
Did you notice that ES 771 BB was unavailable? Is it clear for you?
When you are assigning resources in the drawer, do you need to connect the information about vehicles, trailers and drivers at the same time to make a decision?
In your day to day work, what information would you normally need to know before assigning a vehicle to a trip?
You can see trip TRIP2026-015571 from Argentan on the scheduler assigned to NL 123 13. Please reassign it to vehicle ES 771 BB using right click. Comparing resource assignment with right click and the trip drawer, which felt more natural to you and why?
Once again we need to reassign trip TRIP2026-015571 from Argentan to vehicle ES 771 BB, but this time please use the scheduler to drag and drop the trip onto the vehicle. Of the three ways to assign a vehicle (drawer, right click, drag and drop) which one would you use day to day and why?
TC-08
Assign a Driver to a Trip via Trip Details
📍 Phase 3 · Trip Planner, Trips, Trip Details · Open a trip's detail view and assign an available driver from there
Planner

Objective

Understand how planners assign a driver from the Trip Details page. Test whether the driver assignment option is discoverable, whether availability information is clear, and whether the experience feels consistent with assigning a vehicle.

Starting Point

Trip Planner → Trips section → open first trip in the table

Task Prompt, read aloud

🎙 Say to participant

"Let's go to the Trips menu. Please open the first trip on the table and assign an available driver to it from there. Think out loud as you go."

Success Criteria

Participant opens the Trip Details view
Participant locates the driver assignment option within Trip Details
Participant identifies an available driver and completes the assignment
Participant can confirm the assignment was saved or reflected in the view

Observe

Does the participant find the driver assignment option without guidance?
Is it clear which drivers are available vs unavailable in the list?
Does the participant feel confident choosing a driver, or do they hesitate due to missing information?

Follow up Questions

Was it clear how to find and assign an available driver from the Trip Details page?
Was the availability information shown enough to make a confident choice?
